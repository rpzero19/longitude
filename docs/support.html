Some results weren't picked up

Add them by pasting the report text, and check the review screen before saving — you can remove anything that isn't a result. Very unusual layouts, or a blurry photo, can defeat the reader.

A result was read incorrectly

Always check the review screen against your report before saving. Delete the report from the Reports tab and add it again if something is wrong.

Why do two tests appear as one line?

Labs use different names for the same analyte — SGPT and ALT, PCV and haematocrit. Longitude joins these deliberately, so you get one continuous history instead of fragments.

Why is a value shown in different units to my report?

To compare readings across labs and countries, values are converted to one unit per analyte for the chart. The original value and unit, exactly as your lab printed them, are always shown in the readings list.

Where do the reference ranges come from?

From your own report. Reference intervals are specific to each lab and assay, so Longitude never substitutes a generic range — it uses what your lab printed, and says so.

Does it tell me what my results mean?

No, and this is deliberate. Longitude shows your values and how they compare with your lab's range. Interpreting a result needs your full clinical picture, which an app doesn't have. Talk to your doctor.

Is my data uploaded?

No. There is no account and no server. Reports are read on the device, including by the on-device language model on iPhones that support Apple Intelligence.
